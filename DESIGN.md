# Interactive Teaching Page Design Guide

Design rules for every topic page in the MRCP curriculum. Each page is both the visual for a short YouTube video and a practice page viewers come back to. Carried over from the first page (Choosing the right test) and extended in September 2026 for the 16:9 video stage and the bolder look.

## Core principles

Every page teaches one decision process: show the skeleton first, and let the learner tap for the detail.

1. **One idea per slide.** A slide answers one question in its heading, e.g. "Three quick questions".
2. **Minimal text on screen.** Show names and labels only. Examples, answers and explanations stay hidden until tapped.
3. **No instruction sentences.** Slides never say "Tap a circle…" or "Drag…"; the circles, cards and buckets make the interaction obvious. `build.py` warns when a slide title reads like an instruction.
4. **Hook with a real question.** The first slide after the title is an exam-style stem and its question, with no options or answer. A later slide comes back to it and solves it.
5. **Learn, then practise.** On the candidate's page "Learn" is the recorded video, with a chapter for each slide; the Learn slides themselves live in the presenter's deck, which is only for recording. "Practise" applies the same steps to cases on the page.
6. **Same order everywhere.** Question flows, cases and summaries use one order and one wording.
7. **Every page is illustrated.** Each topic gets figures that picture its own content, so the page is pleasant to look at and each idea has an image to remember it by. Figures:
   - **stand for the topic:** each one shows what its slide is about. In *Types of data*, the concept icons sit above the title; slide 2 shows labels, a podium and a ruler above the three families; slide 3 has an icon beside each question and a chart beside each answer; and the hook, the tree and every case have a cartoon from the recurring cast;
     In *Centre, shape and spread*, the ward's patients stand shoulder to shoulder on their day of discharge, in order, and every middle is found on them; the bell stands over a crowd of adults stacked by height, and the skew over the ward's stays, each crowd dropping in one person at a time before its curve is drawn; a skew stacks its own people (the ward's stays, or eleven births) under a curve that piles against a grey brick wall (a floor at 0 days, a ceiling at 42 weeks) and runs out into a tail;
     In *Standard error*, the same crowd of women stands under its bell in the top strip, a few of them light up as each sample is drawn, and the sample's mean drops as a teal dot onto a second axis below, where the means pile up into a narrower bell;
   - **fill the space the slide already has:** a corner, the room above a label, the side of a card. They never push a teaching slide past the stage, and they shrink, move above their card or hide on small screens;
   - **stay minimal:** few shapes, little or no text, the family colour only where it means something, and neutral blue where colour would hint at an answer;
   - **enter with their content:** a short rise or pop, at the same moment as the content they belong to (see "Entry is quick and in step");
   - **never give an answer away:** a case's figure shows its subject, never its type;
   - **start as mockups:** a new figure is first drawn as three mockups for the author to choose from, and built only once one is chosen.
8. **One set of numbers per topic.** An example keeps the same numbers on every slide it appears on: the hook, the stories, the graphs, the worked tree and the solved case (in *Centre, shape and spread*: mean 6 days, SD 4, median 3; in *Standard error*: 49 women, mean 162 cm, SD 7, SEM 1, 95% CI 160–164 cm). When a slide's own data would give a different number, say so to the author; a mismatch they choose to keep is noted in the YAML beside it.
9. **Order is its own step.** Data first appear in the order they came. Sorting is shown as a separate step, just before whatever needs order (the median, the quartiles); a mean is worked out before any sorting, because it needs none.
10. **One figure builds in one motion.** When a slide moves from one view of the same data to another (a row of people to a stacked graph), the same people move into place and the axis, wall and curve draw in around them; the slide never swaps to a second figure.

## Visual system

A clinical look with conviction: one primary blue, one colour per concept family, big type sized for a 1080p recording.

| Element | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Primary colour | Deep blue (#1F4E8C) for step numbers, stem accents, links, primary buttons and the progress bar |
| Concept families | One family per concept group, each with a background, ink, border and solid shade (coral = categorical, green = ranked, purple = numerical and the people's spread, teal = the mean's precision: the SEM and the 95% CI, grey = neutral) |
| Family use | A concept keeps its colour, label and letter on every slide, card, bucket, choice and flow box. Labels come from the topic's `concepts`, never retyped. A concept with no `letter` shows its icon in its circles instead, and figures write its name, not a symbol (Mean = 6, never x̄ = 6); in *Centre, shape and spread* the mean, median and mode work this way |
| Brand | The logo icon (a G whose bar comes from a heartbeat and a play button, white on a blue tile; see **Logo sizes** below) then the name **MRCP Gafar** (site title in `curriculum.yaml`) in the brand's own lettering (`LETTERS` in `engine/patterns.py`: round strokes like the G; MRCP bold in `--brand-ink`, Gafar light in `--brand`). The pair opens the top bar on every page (so it is on camera in every video) and links back to the home page; in the top bar the icon is 1.3 times the name's height. On the home page the name has the tagline (Inter caps, muted) below it, and the icon beside them runs exactly from the top of MRCP to the foot of the tagline. The icon is every page's tab icon, inlined. The name ends every page title ("Types of data · MRCP Gafar"). A new letter in the name needs its stroke drawn in `LETTERS` first |
| Logo sizes | One icon, two finishes. **Small** (the site's top bar, home page and tab icon; anything under about 96 px): the crisp flat finish, `engine/logo.svg` (also `logo/logo-small.svg`): no shine or shadow, a deep two-tone blue tile, a bright teal play and a heavy heartbeat, so it stays sharp down to 16 px. **Large** (the YouTube profile picture, video intros, social posts; 96 px and up): the glossy finish, `logo/logo-large.svg`: lit from above, a glass sheen, a soft teal glow and a raised glyph. Exports live in `logo/` (`youtube-800.png` is full-bleed square, because YouTube crops it to a circle). The pages carry only the small finish; the shape never changes between them |
| Title rule | A single-colour gradient under the page title and the home page heading: primary blue fading to transparent |
| Headings | Outfit Medium (500): a round geometric sans that echoes the logo's lettering. It is embedded at that one weight, so every heading, card label and big number drawn in the heading face (`--serif`, a name kept from the old serif) renders Medium, never bolder. On the stage: title 80 px, slide heading 44 px |
| Body text | Sans-serif (Inter). On the stage: 20 px body, 23 px stems, 16 px small text; contrast at least 4.5:1 |
| Small labels | Uppercase, letter-spaced, 13 px on the stage, muted or family ink |
| No eyebrows | No kicker or label above a heading: the heading carries the slide. The top bar reads: wordmark, specialty, topic, then the part and slide name |
| Cards | 16 px corners, thin border, soft offset shadow, a 4–6 px coloured **top** edge for the family. No thick coloured left borders |
| Circles | Outlined in the family colour; filled solid with a soft ring when selected |
| Feedback | Green for correct, red for wrong, each with a drawn tick or cross icon (never a text glyph) |
| Special states | Amber marks anything unscored, such as the solved example |
| Themes | Light by default (recording), dark as a toggle; every colour is a light and a dark token |
| Fonts and assets | Embedded in the file, so the page works offline |
| Graphs of a distribution | Both axes: the value along the bottom and the share of people up the side (% of people, in round tens: 0, 10, 20, 30%) |
| Neutral items | Things with no concept of their own stay neutral: readings other than the mean take greys (darker nearer the point that matters), and no new hue or colour flash appears during a working |
| Figures | Our own SVG drawings in `content/figures/`, inlined by the build (no image files, no stock art). Two kinds: **diagrams** (scales, charts) drawn in the neutral primary blue and greys, never in a concept's family colour where that would hint at an answer; and **scenes**, flat cartoon characters with a dark outline (`--sc-line`, lighter in dark mode), fixed skin, hair and uniform colours, and a recurring cast (the patient, the nurse and the doctor) so the slides read as one story. A figure pops in once as its slide opens. On phones a scene moves above its card |

## Interaction patterns

Each slide is one pattern, named in the topic's YAML (`pattern:`). Pick the one that matches the shape of the content.

| Pattern (`pattern:`) | Use it for | What the learner does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title (`title`, automatic) | Every page | Taps Learn (play icon on the candidate's page: the video; open-book icon in the presenter's deck: the slides) or Practise (pencil icon: the cases). The title, its rule (fading out at both ends) and the two cards are centred. With `cover: concepts`, each concept's icon sits in a row above the title in its family colour, rising in one by one |
| Video (`video`, automatic) | Learn on the candidate's page | Sees the player on the left and a chapter for each Learn slide on the right (under the player on phones). The player is a drawn poster (the concept icons, the topic's name, a white play button on the primary blue) until tapped, so nothing loads from YouTube and no cookie is set before then; the tap loads the privacy-enhanced player (youtube-nocookie) and plays. A chapter with a time (the slide's `at`, m:ss) plays from there, or seeks the player once it is in. Before the video is recorded (`video: null`) the poster is grey and dashed, reads Video coming soon, and the chapters are listed by number with no times, as a preview. Leaving the slide pauses the video. The player is capped by the height under the bars, so it never pushes the slide past the stage |
| Hook (`hook`) | The opening exam question | Reads it; nothing to tap. Optional `figure` (a diagram inside the card, such as the pain scale) and `scene` (a cartoon beside the card) |
| Tap-to-reveal circles (`spectrum`) | A set of types or categories on a spectrum | Taps a circle; its examples appear beneath, one by one. A group's `figure` puts a small picture of what the family means above its name (labels in no order, a podium, a ruler), in the family colour. As the slide opens each family rises as one (its picture, its name and its circles together), left to right. The circles stand on their own, with no line joining them |
| Zoom circle (`spectrum` with `open: zoom`) | A spectrum whose examples deserve the whole slide | Taps a circle; the rest of the slide fades back and the circle grows out of its dot into a large centred circle holding its letter, its name (large serif, family ink) and its examples as soft pills (white, thin family border, family dot, dark ink; popping in one by one; sized as a share of the circle so they scale with it; a long name stays on one line at a smaller size, and a long list takes smaller pills, so nothing spills out of the circle), over a fine dot texture that shows only near the rim and a thin dashed inner ring. Tapping outside, or Esc, shrinks it back into its dot; tapping another dot swaps to that one |
| Sequential question flow (`question-flow`) | A short yes/no decision (2–4 questions) | Answers each question in turn; the next unlocks and the "No" result appears. Boxes show their question or name only |
| Decision tree (`decision-tree`) | A short yes/no decision taught top-down (2–4 questions) | The first question (an outlined blue pill) sits at the top with Yes and No centred beneath it. Each later question pops in first, then its Yes and No fade in beneath it. The chosen answer slides onto the centre line, pushing the other aside. Yes draws a green line down to the next question; No draws a coral line down to the final answer. The final answer is unique: a solid, glowing family-colour card with the letter in a white circle, the name in white serif and a faint dot texture, popping in. The other answer re-routes the branch and tapping a chosen answer again undoes it; a stray click leaves the answers as they are. The tree grows downward, so nothing above moves. With `examples` (per answer: a list, or labelled sub-lists), tapping the final answer grows a zoom circle out of its letter; tapping it again, or anywhere else, closes the circle and leaves the tree as it was. A step's `figure` puts a small neutral-blue icon of what the question means beside its pill (hidden on phones); `charts` draws, beside each answer as it appears, the chart that type of data is shown with, in the answer's family colour (under the card on phones and tablets); `scene` puts a cartoon (such as the thinking doctor) in the bottom-left corner of the stage, above the tree on phones and tablets. **In rows** (every step has `at: [row, column]`): each step or final takes its cell as the trail reaches it; either answer can end on a final (`yes:` or `no:` a concept, shown in the cell under its step), go on to the next step (the default), or skip ahead (`no: {to: 3}`); no lines join the cells: a small arrow disc in the gap after each chosen answer points to where it leads (down, or across), and each question has a clear gap above its Yes and No |
| Live worked tree (`decision-tree` with `stem`) | Solving the hook in front of the viewer | The stem sits beside the tree (above it on phones and portrait tablets); the presenter answers Yes or No live, as in the decision tree. With `path` (the right answers) and `wrong` (one line per step), a wrong answer turns red, shakes, says "Not quite." and why beneath it, and the branch goes no further until the right answer is chosen. `side` lists figures under the stem: the first shows at the start, the next after each answer, and the last one stays. With no `side`, a `scene` (such as the thinking doctor) stands under the stem instead |
| Worked flow (`question-flow` with `path` and `stem`) | Solving the hook with the flow | Taps each question; only the branch taken lights, and the answer it lands on stands out |
| Stays chart (`dot-plot`) | A few values and the middles found from them | Each value is a person standing on their day; people who share a day stand shoulder to shoulder, so the whole row reads in order from left to right. Every whole number in the main run of values has a slot, and a far-out value sits after a break in the axis (//). Taps a measure's circle (mean, median or mode) and sees how it is found on the people, its working written on its marker line, never in a line of text beneath: the mean pulses everyone, a fulcrum slides to the balance point and the marker reads Mean = 66 ÷ 11 = 6; the median fades people in pairs from both ends until the middle one is left, then a dashed box holds each half with its count (5 and 5) and the marker reads Median = 3; the mode lights the people who share the commonest day and reads Mode = 2. The measures are worked out from `values`, never typed. With `swap`, one person (dashed outline) moves to another day when tapped and back when tapped again, and the open measure follows, so one long stay is seen to move the mean and not the median. One measure open at a time; a click anywhere else returns the slide to its start |
| Shapes on one graph (`curve` with `steps`) | What normal and skewed mean, before any spread | Taps a shape's circle; it draws on the one graph on a real axis, in its family colour, with a y axis for the share of people (% of people, in round tens: 0, 10, 20, 30%). Its people drop onto the axis one at a time, in a random order, each onto the top of its own stack; once the last has landed the curve draws over them, then the middles, the order row and the examples follow. No taps on the graph. **Normal** (`crowd`): a crowd of adults in columns by height under a bell, one pill on its line naming all three with the value (Mean = Median = Mode = 162 cm). **Skewed from people** (`values`, `sigma`, and `ceiling` for negative skew): the people stack on their values (the stays chart, with `breaks: false` to keep one unbroken axis) against a grey brick wall that stands from the start (at 0 for positive skew, at `ceiling` for negative, named by `wall_label`), under a fitted log-normal curve that runs out into a tail named by `tail_label`; the mean, the median and the mode land as lines with pills (Mean 6, Median 3, Mode 2) stepping down above the curve, worked out from the people. (A skewed step without `values` draws the curve alone, from `median`, `sigma` and `range`.) Their order is written beneath (Mode = Median = Mean, or with <), with the step's `summary`, if it has one, at the end of the same row, and the examples follow as pills. One shape at a time |
| Spread panels (`curve` with `panels`) | SD and IQR, side by side or one alone | Taps a chip in a panel; a single panel fills the width. An `sd` panel stands the crowd of heights in columns under the bell with a y axis (% of people), has the mean as a dotted line with its value, and shades (with dashed edges, the people beyond them faded) ±1, ±2 or ±3 SD out from it, the share inside and the range beneath. A `dots` panel is the stays chart, stacked on the days against a wall at 0 under the curve they make (`sigma`), and labels each spread on the figure above the curve: the IQR lights everyone from the lower quartile to the upper inside a box under a bracket (IQR 2 to 6 days), the range brackets everyone, the range is a bold bar from the smallest to the largest, and mean ± 1 SD or ± 2 SD (the panel's stated `sd` if it has one, else the people's own in whole units: 6 ± 4, 6 ± 2 × 4) is a band standing over the stays from low to high, with the mean dashed through it; where it goes below zero the band runs red through the wall, onto a dashed stretch of axis, to the impossible value. Which spread a chip shows comes from its words (IQR, Range, Mean ± 1 SD, Mean ± 2 SD). An `sd` panel's chip can also be Mean ± 2 SEM (with the panel's `sem`): a narrow teal band about the mean, nearly all the people faded beyond it, set against Mean ± 2 SD (the reference range against the 95% CI); `pct` is read out below and `tags` (if given) label the band on the figure. An `sem` panel (`mean`, `sd`, `unit`, `axis`, `means_axis`, chips `n = 4`, `n = 16` …) is the sampling story's figure with a curve in place of the dots: the people's bell stays put while each chip slides the means' bell to its n, narrower and taller, its half-width reading SEM = 7 ÷ √49 = 1 cm. The panel's summary card (Mean (SD), Median (IQR)), if it has one, appears with its first chip |
| Story (`story`) | Where a measure or idea came from, told in the video | A cartoon `scene` sits beside one figure (the scene above it on phones); each tap on the figure adds the next beat, in step with the narration. The story itself is spoken, never written on the slide: the slide shows only its title, the figure and the working on the figure's markers. A scene's parts can join (`data-beat`) or leave (`data-until`) at a beat. Each `kind` draws its own beats and works every number out from the slide's data: **mean** (`values`): readings drop onto a number line one by one, the one on the mean dark and the others in two greys, one for each distance from it (9 and 11, 8 and 12); then the sum is written and the mean lands on the star with its working, its answer on a gold pill, the star's colour (Mean = 50 ÷ 5 = 10; the mean's answer sits on this pill in every story); **median** (the same `values`, `outlier: {from, to: [far, farther]}`): the readings stand in a row over a number line in the order they were taken, each value written under its dot; the mean lands on the line with its working (a mean needs no order); with the storm, one reading turns wild (12 to 40), grows bold and runs to the far end of the line past a break (//), then wilder (400) and bolder still, the mean following each time; only then do the others line up in order and every reading falls onto its value on the line; last, the readings drop out in pairs from both ends to leave the median on the star, the mean far off; **iqr** (`values` in the order they came, 4k + 3 of them, and `unit`): the people stand evenly spaced in one row, each with their value over their head, then line up in order; the middle one lights up as the median (Median = 3) with a dashed box round the half on each side, the middle of each half lights up as a quartile (Q1 = 2, Q3 = 6), a box between the quartiles holds the middle half (IQR 2 to 6 days) while the far value pulses outside it, then how a paper writes it (Median 3 (IQR 2–6)); with a `panel` (a `dots` spread panel, as below) a last beat builds the graph in the same figure: the people step onto their days and stack up, the axis, wall and curve appear, and the panel's chips and summary card come up beneath; **sampling** (`population: {shape: normal or positive, mean, sd}`, `n`, `unit`, `axis`, `means_axis`, and `wall_label` for a skew): one figure in two strips on one value axis. The top strip is the population: 20 people stacked under its curve (the heights crowd of *Centre, shape and spread*, or a skew's people at its quantiles against a wall at 0), a y axis of % of people, the mean dotted with its pill, and the SD drawn as the curve's half-width (a line from the mean to one SD out, at the curve's height there, named SD 7 cm). The bottom strip holds the means of samples of `n`, with a y axis of % of samples. Beats: a few of the crowd light up teal as a sample and its mean drops as a teal dot onto the lower axis, with its value; a second sample; then many more (40 in all), piling up in bins half an SEM wide; last, the curve the means make is drawn with its half-width worked out (SEM = 7 ÷ √49 = 1 cm). The means sit at the quantiles of their own distribution (normal for a bell, gamma for a skew, so a skewed population still gives a near-normal pile); **mode** (`counts`, `unit`, `categories`, `categories_unit`): people stack on their shoe size, the mean is a size nobody wears (a crossed-out shoe), the tallest stack is the mode, then the same people as blood groups, where Mean and Median are struck out and only the mode is named; **coin** (`tosses`, `claim`): H₀ (the coin is fair) on an olive pill; the coins land one by one, all heads, each over the chance of heads every time so far as a stacked fraction (1/2 … 1/1,024); then all heads or all tails, 2 in 1,024; then p = 2 ÷ 1,024 ≈ 0.002 on an olive pill; **gap** (`sd`, `n` in each group, `gap`, `means` [placebo, drug], `means_axis`, three `labels`, `unit`, `axis`, `y_axis`): one tap per step. It opens on the placebo group's mean systolic BP as a grey bell on its own axis (Systolic BP, the mean dashed down to its value, 140 mmHg, SEM 1.1 marked, dashed lines 2 SEM either side with the name and "2 SEM" just outside the outer one; the lines stay on each bell when the two join and when the drug slides back); the drug group's teal bell, the same width, round its own mean (136 mmHg) on a row below, to the left; names and "2 SEM" stand outside each pair (the drug's on the left, the placebo's on the right); the gap is placebo − drug; the two join on one axis, overlapping, with the gap bracketed (Gap 4 mmHg); three trials, one per tap, each its own small pair of bells round its two means with its gap bracketed (Gap 2.9, 5.1, 3.6) and dropped as a dot onto an axis below, the one before fading; many more trials' gaps pile up there (60 in all, at the quantiles of their bell, in bins of 0.5) under a dashed outline with SE 1.5 marked, a bell wider than either mean's; then the drug bell slides back onto the placebo's (the drug does nothing); three trials again, one per tap, gaps either side of 0 (1.2, −1.0, 0.6); their pile round 0, the same width; then, on its own tap, the pile becomes the bell of gaps that chance alone would give (both axes, only 0 and the drug's side labelled, the SE marked with a dashed drop to the axis); the dashed 2 SE lines with 2.5% shaded beyond each; the trial's gap lands as a small dot with its sum above the bell (4 ÷ 1.5 = 2.7 SEs, teal); last, the tails at least that far out, true to scale but edged thick in olive, fill in with 0.4% each, and the sum's place holds p = 0.4% + 0.4% ≈ 0.008; **line** (`cut`, `trials: [{label, p}]`, optional `origin`): a ruler of p-values on a log scale (1 at the top, 0.001 at the foot); with an `origin` the ruler stands to the right and a small bell comes first on the left, its tails beyond the dashed 2 SE lines in olive (2.5% each), an olive pill 5% = 1 in 20, and who chose the line beneath (Fisher, 1925); then the dashed line at 0.05 = 1 in 20 in olive with the significant side below it shaded and the two sides named from the sig and ns concepts; then each trial lands on the ruler at its p as a chip; **grid** (`sd`, `gap`, `ns` [one or two group sizes] with a `label` each; one trial also needs a `heading` and `unit`, two a `cut` and optional `origin`): p as the trial run 1,000 times with placebo in both groups, 1,000 grey squares in 40 columns of 25, one per run, lined up by its gap, the biggest fall first and the biggest rise last. With one trial (*Hypothesis testing*: What does p = 0.008 mean? and What does p = 0.4 mean?) it opens on the grid under its heading (1,000 trials · placebo in both groups); then the runs with a gap at least the trial's, either way, light up in olive at both ends, outside in, with the count at each end beneath (4 · ≥ 4 mmHg lower, ≥ 4 mmHg higher · 4); then how many in 1,000 and p on an olive pill beside it (8 in 1,000, p = 0.008; 400 in 1,000, p = 0.4). The counts are worked out from the SE of the difference (SD × √(2 ÷ n)), two-sided, so they are expected numbers (said as "about"). With two trials (Where is the line?) both grids open side by side under their labels, already lit, each with its count and p beneath; the cut's columns at each end stand a little apart, and the next tap shades them with a dashed olive line just inside (25 at each end for 0.05), the olive pill 50 in 1,000 = 1 in 20 = 0.05 beneath, and who drew it beside it (Fisher, 1925); then each trial's verdict, one per tap, on a pill under its grid (8 < 50 · Significant in olive, 400 > 50 · Not significant in grey); **ncompare** (`sd`, `gap`, `ns` [two group sizes], two `labels`, `axis`, `y_axis`): the same gap in two trials side by side under their headings (400 people, 40 people); one row per tap: each trial's SE worked out on a teal pill (15 × √(2 ÷ 200) = 1.5; 15 × √(2 ÷ 20) ≈ 4.7); each trial's chance bell round 0 with both axes, true to scale so the smaller trial's is wider and lower, the gap landing on each; how many SEs out the gap is (2.7, 0.85); last, the tails beyond the gap fill in olive and each p appears on an olive pill (0.008, 0.4); **ci** (`sd`, `n`, `gap`, two `labels`, `axis`, `y_axis`): our trial's bell of gaps (SE of the difference wide) round what we found, with both axes (only 0 and the drug's side labelled) and our gap as a dot; then the 2 SE lines and its middle 95% shaded; then the CI drops out beneath as a teal bar with its sum on a teal pill (95% CI = 4 ± 2 × 1.5 = 1 to 7); last, chance's grey bell round 0 beside it with its own 95% as a grey bar beneath (−3 to +3): 0 lies outside our CI just as our gap lies outside chance's; with `chance: false` it stops at our CI (*Confidence intervals and p*: Where could the true effect be?); **slide** (`panels`, each with `none`, `axis`, `range`, `ticks`, `at`, a `label` when there are two, and `se` for a difference, or `half` or `factor` for a ratio): one panel filling the width, or two, a difference (no effect = 0) and a ratio (no effect = 1), each with its dashed no-effect line; a ratio with a `factor` sits on a log axis, its CI from c ÷ factor to c × factor (0.6: 0.48 to 0.75); one teal CI per panel slides towards it, one place per tap, difference first, its p on an olive pill just right of the line: a difference's p worked out from its SE (0.008, 0.05, 0.3), a ratio's read from where its CI stands (p < 0.05 clear of 1, p = 0.05 touching, p > 0.05 across); **samples** (`sd`, `means` [two samples], `axis`, `y_axis`): people's values in one sample as a bell (both axes, % of people) with ± SD shaded purple and its mean written over the peak (Mean 140); then a second sample beside it, the first bell dashed behind it, its mean lit teal (Mean 141), the same SD; then a purple pill beneath: Means differ · SD stays 15; **means** (`sd`, `n`, `means` [the two samples' means], `axis`, `y_axis`): an axis of means (135 to 145) with % of samples; it opens on the two means as grey dots, ringed; then many more samples of n pile up (60 in all, in bins of 0.5, one dot per sample) and the teal bell they make is drawn with its width marked (an arrow, SEM 1.1) and its sum beside it (15 ÷ √200 ≈ 1.1); **gaps** (`sd`, `n`, `pairs` [1 to 5 trials, each [mean, mean]], `means_axis`, `axis`, `y_axis`): trials with both groups on placebo, one per tap. On the left, the trial's two group means as bells SEM wide, overlapping on one small axis (light grey and dark grey, each mean dashed), the gap bracketed above them (Gap 1, −1.6, 2.3); each trial replaces the one before and its gap drops, ringed, onto an axis of gaps on the right (−6 to 6, minus signs kept, a dashed line at 0, % of trials), under a grey arrow. ## Behaviour rules

Every interactive element behaves the same way, so learners never have to relearn the page.

- **Tap again to close.** Tapping an open item hides it.
- **Click anywhere to clear, but only what one tap brings back.** A click elsewhere on the slide closes an open card, circle, chip or measure. A figure built up over several taps (a story, a worked sum, a decision tree, a question flow) keeps its state until the slide is left: a story and a worked sum have small Back and Next buttons in their top-right corner to step it either way; a tree and a flow step back by tapping an answered step again. Clicks inside an opened list or card keep it open. Not every click counts: a drag or scroll, a click on the top or bottom bar, and a near miss (within 24 px of a control on the slide) close nothing.
- **One open item per group.** Opening a circle closes its neighbour; reveal cards in a group open one at a time.
- **Entry is quick and in step.** When a slide opens, a figure and the content it belongs to (a family's picture, its name and its circles) enter together, with the same start and length. Groups follow each other left to right or top to bottom about 0.16 s apart, each entrance lasts about 0.5 s, and the whole slide has settled within about 0.9 s: slow enough to be seen, quick enough that nobody waits on the slide. Every entrance on every slide uses this one timing (0.5 s each, 0.16 s apart, from 0.1 s): the title icons, the family pictures and circles, the pain scale, and the pieces of every cartoon. Content revealed by a tap follows "Reveal in sequence" instead.
- **Reading order.** Things appear in the order they are read: a question before its answers, a heading before its content. When a new step opens, its Yes and No wait until the question has landed.
- **One control per job.** Two buttons never do the same thing on screen. A solved case has no Next of its own: the bar's Next changes its words ("Next case", "See your score") and lights up instead.
- **Lines only where they mean something.** A line shows a route or a link, such as a tree branch from a question to its answer. Circles, cards and pictures are not joined by decorative lines. A tree laid out in rows uses no lines at all: a small arrow disc in the gap points the way, and each question has clear space above its Yes and No.
- **Re-entering a slide starts fresh.** Every pattern resets when its slide is shown again.
- **Only the tapped item grows.** Neighbouring cards keep their size; nothing stretches around empty space.
- **Reveal in sequence.** Lists appear one line after another (about 0.2 s apart); motion is off for users who prefer reduced motion.
- **Only the target is clickable.** In circle groups, only the circle responds, not its label.
- **No placeholders, no sub-labels.** Hidden answers leave no "?" marks; circles, cards, buckets and choices show only their name (and the concept's letter).
- **Cases are dealt in a random order.** Practise (or New cases) reshuffles the cases, numbers them by their place in the run, keeps the solved example first and restarts the score.
- **A page opens on its title.** The candidate's page always opens on Learn and Practise, whatever was open last (a reload keeps the video open, never a case). The presenter's deck reopens where it was left.
- **Practise offers to carry on.** Each run is kept in the browser: the cases' order, every answer and the slide last shown. When a run was left with a scored case still to do, Practise asks "Continue where you left off?" over the title, with the score so far, and two buttons: Continue (the same cases in the same order, every answer and the score back as they were, on the slide last shown) and Start again (a fresh deal at 0). A tap outside the card, or Esc, closes it. A finished run is not offered.
- **Choices sit under the question.** A case's choices are centred under its stem card, not under the cartoon, each a quarter of the card's width (a half when the page reflows), so a short last row sits in the middle.
- **Answer positions are shuffled.** Each case fixes its own option order from its ID, so the answer is not always first and the order is the same every time that case is opened.
- **A case gives nothing away.** The stem, question and choices carry no hints; the clue phrase is highlighted only after a wrong answer (and in the solved example).
- **Wrong answers teach.** A wrong choice shakes, gives a one-line hint and lights the clue phrase. Scoring counts the first attempt only, once per case.
- **A case has a cartoon.** One cast member with one prop for the case's subject (a painkiller, a cigarette, a walking stick), never its type, sits beside the stem card, and above it on phones.
- **A right answer explains.** It shows why, and lights the bar's Next with a soft pulse. On a case the bar's Next reads "Next case" (or "See your score" after the last case); there is no second Next inside the case. In a multi-step case, a correct step moves on by itself after about 0.75 s.
- **Learn and Practise are separate paths.** Next, Back and the arrow keys never cross from one into the other: each part's first slide has no Back and its last slide has no Next, and the slide count and progress bar count within the part. Home returns to the title.
- **Navigation is always there.** Back, Home and Next sit in a bottom bar, the arrow keys move between slides, and swipes do the same on touch screens (on a phone held sideways, swipes replace the bar; see below). The title slide has none of them. A hidden button takes no space. In the recording view the tray holds Back and Next instead (see below).
- **Hidden slides.** A slide with `hidden: true` stays in the YAML and is still checked, but is left out of the page.
- **Keyboard and screen readers.** Every clickable element is a button or has a button role, is reachable with Tab, and opens with Enter or Space.
- **Every event goes through `record()`.** Views and answers are reported with permanent IDs through one function in the engine, so analytics and a results database can plug in later.

## Layout and responsiveness

- **The 16:9 stage.** In any landscape window at least 900 × 480 px (laptops, desktops, tablets held sideways), the page is a canvas at least 1280 × 720, scaled to fit and widened (or deepened) to the window's shape, so it fills the whole window with no letterbox. A 16:9 window gets exactly 1280 × 720, so every recording frames the same, and a 1920 × 1080 recording is the stage at 1.5×.
- **Fluid below that, and in portrait.** On phones, small windows and portrait tablets the page reflows and scrolls, with a sticky top and bottom bar and 16 px side margins. The window is measured by its real width (`clientWidth`), because a tablet widens `innerWidth` to fit the canvas.
- **A phone held sideways.** On a touch screen in landscape below the stage size, the bottom bar goes to give the slide room. Swiping left moves to the next slide and swiping right moves back (swipes work on every touch screen). Home and the slide count move into the top bar, the part label and specialty drop out of it, and a tree's cartoon is hidden.
- **Tablets are roomy, not shrunk.** A fluid page 601–1099 px wide uses a larger size unit, so type, dots and spacing stay near their stage size instead of shrinking with the width. Tablet layouts use the extra height: the decision tree gets longer links and bigger steps, question flows stack vertically, answer choices sit 2 × 2, warm-up buckets 2 × 2 and reveal cards three across.
- **Sizes are written once.** Engine sizes use `clamp(phone, n * var(--u), stage)`, where `--u` is 1% of the page width (1.4% on tablets), so they sit at their stage value on the canvas and shrink on phones. Never write a bare `cqw` size. Responsive rules use container queries on the page, not media queries.
- **A teaching slide fits the stage** with its answers open: no vertical scrolling. Shrink cards and spacing rather than letting it scroll. Practice slides (warm-up, end) may scroll.
- **Short slides are centred:** the heading stays at the top and the content sits in the middle of the space below it (hook, question flows, reveal cards, zoom spectra, end).
- **Question flows** run left to right on the stage and turn vertical on phones and portrait tablets.
- **Bottom bar on phones:** buttons show an icon only, except Next.

## Presenter's deck

Every topic builds twice from the same YAML: the candidate's page (`dist/<specialty>/<slug>.html`: title, video, Practise) and the presenter's deck (`presenter/<specialty>/<slug>.html`: title, every Learn slide, Practise). The deck is built on the presenter's own computer only: `presenter/` is ignored by git, so it is never published. `presenter/index.html` lists every deck, and a deck's wordmark goes back to that list. Every design rule for Learn slides still applies to it, since the slides are what the video shows.

## Recording view

The presenter's mode for recording a video on a tablet with a stylus (built for a Galaxy Tab S10 FE and its S Pen), in the presenter's deck only. It opens from the screen button in the top bar (full screen), from the P key, or from a link ending `?present`; the top-bar button shows on tablets either way up and on computers, and is hidden on phones, windows under 600 px on their short side, and the candidate's page.

- **A clean 16:9 frame.** The slide is an exact 1280 × 720 frame at the top of the screen, so a recording crops to a clean 16:9. The top bar (wordmark, topic, score) and the progress bar stay; the bottom bar goes, and the slide moves down by half its height, so the content and any zoom circle sit centred below the top bar. The tray fills the strip below it (at least 56 px; on a 16:10 tablet the strip left over).
- **The tray** (one row, in the letterbox colour): Back, the slide count and Next on the left; pen, highlighter, one colour circle (a tap opens a ring of colours: eight for the pen, six soft ones for the highlighter; each tool keeps its own), eraser, undo and clear in the middle; whole slide (zoom out) and leave on the right.
- **The pen only writes.** A stylus draws on every part of the slide and never opens, closes or swipes anything. A press of its side button while it hovers selects the eraser, and the next press the pen (whatever was in hand before). Holding the button while writing does nothing: Chrome on the Galaxy Tab does not report the pen on the glass with the button held reliably (tried in September 2026). The button must be seen up for 0.15 s before a press counts again, since what the pen reports can flicker while it is held. Fingers and the mouse work the slide as usual, and one finger dragged up or down scrolls it (the page scrolls it itself: the browser is never allowed to pan, since a pan would cut a pen stroke off). A touch while the pen is on or near the screen (within 0.6 s) is a resting palm and does nothing.
- **Pen and highlighter.** The pen is a 4 px line; a tap on the pen button while the pen is in hand opens a small panel above it with four sizes (2, 4, 7 and 11 px, each a dot of its width). A choice, the same tap again, or a tap anywhere else (the slide and the pen on it included) closes a pop-up. The highlighter is a wide band in its colour, blended under the text so the words stay sharp. The eraser rubs out only the ink under it, like a real one, cutting a stroke it crosses in two; one undo puts back everything a single rub took. Undo steps back through strokes, rubbing out and clearing.
- **Ink belongs to its slide.** It goes when the slide changes and comes back when the slide is shown again. It is kept for the session only.
- **Zoom.** Two fingers pinch in (up to 4×) and out and move the slide about; the ink zooms with the slide, and new ink written while zoomed keeps its on-screen width. Changing slide, or the whole-slide button, returns to the full slide.
